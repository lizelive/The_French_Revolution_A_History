# Realized Ideals (Or: How Monarchies Rot)

Let's zoom out from Louis's sick room for a second and look at the BIG PICTURE. Because what's happening here isn't just about one gross old king dying of smallpox—it's about an entire SYSTEM collapsing.

## The Good Old Days (That Were Actually Terrible)

Once upon a time, you could literally MAKE a king. Find a guy, dress him up fancy, tell everyone he's special, and boom—instant monarchy. People would actually OBEY him. They'd watch him ride through the streets in an elaborate carriage, surrounded by his mistress and her massive collection of toiletries (yes, really—royal mistresses traveled with their own makeup wagons), and they'd think: "Ah yes, our conquering hero."

This actually worked for centuries! Humans are weird like that. We'll believe almost anything if enough people act like it's true. Even African tribes knew this trick—take some sticks and old clothes, arrange them just right, and suddenly you've got a god to worship. Europeans weren't any different; they just used fancier sticks.

## Meanwhile, Across the Atlantic...

But wait! What's that sound? It's the sound of DEMOCRACY being born, and it sounds suspiciously like rifle fire from Bunker Hill (1775) and the Boston Tea Party (1773)!

That's right—while Louis lies dying, the American colonies are telling Britain to get wrecked, and the whole concept of "divine right of kings" is getting a serious reality check. The future has arrived, and it's bringing revolutionary ideas about equality and self-governance.

(Cue the Star-Spangled Banner played on a kazoo.)

## A Brief Tour of Dead French Kings

Want to feel mortal? Let's speedrun through some former rulers of France:

- **The Merovingians**: Gone. They used to roll through Paris in ox-carts with their fancy long hair. Now? Dust.
- **Charlemagne**: "Sleeping" in Salzburg, waiting to wake up like some medieval King Arthur. *Spoiler: He's not waking up.*
- **Charles the Hammer**: Remember when that name inspired fear? No? Neither does anyone else.
- **All those murderous medieval queens** (Fredegonda, Brunhilda, etc.): Dead. Their palace intrigues are forgotten.

Everything. Dies. Including political systems.

## But Hey, At Least They Built Cool Stuff

Let's give credit where it's due. Paris went from a muddy river crossing to a major European capital. There are cathedrals! Palaces! A legal system! Libraries with actual books! Ships that sail by the stars! The French invented and collected a LOT of cool stuff over the centuries.

All of this represents what we might call "realized ideals"—the concrete achievements of civilization.

## The Two Big Ideas: Church and King

Two institutions dominated medieval life:

**The Church** was EVERYTHING. Even in the remotest mountain village, there was a little church where people could feel connected to something bigger. It gave meaning to life and death. It was worth dying for—and killing for.

**The Kingship** was about concentrating power in "the strongest man." "King" literally comes from words meaning "the one who CAN"—the able one. In theory, you'd pick your best warrior, put him on a shield, and swear loyalty. In practice, it became hereditary and increasingly corrupt.

Both ideas kind of made sense once. A thousand years of French history led to Louis XIV confidently declaring "I AM the State" (*L'État, c'est moi*) without anyone laughing in his face.

But that was the PEAK. It's all downhill from there.

## The Current Situation (It's Bad)

Here's where things stand in 1774:

**The Church**: Basically a zombie. The Sorbonne (France's premier theological university) mumbles incoherently while actual intellectual leadership has passed to the *philosophes*—Voltaire, Rousseau, Diderot, and their encyclopedia-writing friends. The people doing the real thinking are explicitly ANTI-church.

**The Kingship**: Louis XV's main occupation? Hunting. When there's no hunt scheduled, the court calendar literally reads: "Today His Majesty will do NOTHING." The king has become an expensive ornament.

**The Nobles**: Remember when aristocrats were warriors? Now they're just fashion accessories who spend their time dressing fancy and angling for court favors. Their most important remaining "virtue" is a willingness to fight duels over perceived slights. That's it. That's the whole job.

**The People**: Getting absolutely CRUSHED. They're taxed into poverty, drafted into wars that don't benefit them, and treated like livestock. In Brittany, peasants once rioted against the introduction of pendulum clocks because they thought it was somehow connected to another tax. That's how jumpy and desperate they were.

And here's a fun story: In 1750, Paris police were caught kidnapping children from respectable families to extort ransom money. When the mothers started screaming in the streets, a rumor spread that the children were being harvested for *blood baths* to cure some nobleman's syphilis. Some rioters were hanged to quiet things down. The police continued as before.

THIS is the France of 1774. A powder keg with a dying king on top.

## The New Powers Rising

But here's the thing: as the old system rots, new forces are emerging:

1. **The Lawyers**: They have their own nobility now and are gaining power through the courts.
2. **The Merchants**: Money talks, even if the aristocracy pretends not to hear.
3. **The Writers**: This is the big one. A new "nobility of literature" has emerged—people with no swords and no money, but with IDEAS. And ideas, it turns out, are the most dangerous things of all.

French Enlightenment philosophy has arrived. Faith is out; skepticism is in. Nobody believes the lies anymore, but nobody knows what to replace them with either. Into that vacuum will rush... well, we'll see.

## The Bottom Line

Lord Chesterfield, writing in 1753 (twenty years before Louis XV's death), observed:

> "All the symptoms which I have ever met with in History, previous to great Changes and Revolutions in government, now exist and daily increase in France."

The man was a prophet. The fuse has been lit. The powder is dry.

Something is about to explode.
